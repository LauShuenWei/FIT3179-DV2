Where, When and Under What Conditions Is Wildlife Observed?

Citizen-science observations provide a valuable view of when and where wildlife is recorded across Australia. This visualisation explores how recording patterns differ across animal groups, locations, seasons and climate conditions.

02

Different Animals, Different Places

Birds, mammals, reptiles and amphibians show different recording patterns across Australian states and across the landscape.

Birds Dominate Australia’s Wildlife Records

Share of wildlife observation records by animal group, Australia, 2018–2024

Birds account for nearly three-quarters of all wildlife observation records in this dataset. Reptiles and mammals each contribute around one-tenth, while amphibians represent the smallest share. These proportions reflect recording activity rather than relative wildlife population abundance.

Animal Groups Contribute Differently Across States

Share of each animal group's recorded observations by Australian state or territory, 2018–2024

The dashed line marks an equal 12.5% share across Australia's eight states and territories. Queensland contributes the largest shares of mammal, reptile and amphibian records, while Victoria leads bird records. These percentages describe recording patterns rather than wildlife abundance.

Wildlife Groups Form Different Recording Hotspots

Top 250 recording grid cells by animal group, Australia, 2018–2024

The maps compare the 250 most frequently recorded 0.5° grid cells within each animal group. Larger circles represent stronger recording hotspots relative to the most-recorded cell for that group. Birds and mammals show widespread hotspots, while amphibian recording is more concentrated toward eastern and south-eastern Australia. Hover over a circle for details.

03

Wildlife Through the Seasons

Observation activity also changes through the year. Comparing months, seasons and years reveals when different animal groups are most often recorded.

Reptiles and Amphibians Show the Strongest Seasonal Swings

Monthly share of recorded observations within each animal group, Australia, 2018–2024

Reptile and amphibian recording activity shows the strongest seasonal variation, falling during winter before rising sharply through spring. Bird and mammal observations are more evenly distributed across the year. Hover over a month for exact observation counts and monthly shares.

Seasonal Recording Shares Stay Mostly Stable Over Time

Seasonal share of recorded observations by year and animal group, Australia, 2018–2024

Seasonal shares remain fairly stable across 2018–2024. Spring generally contributes the largest share of all wildlife records, while reptiles and amphibians show stronger seasonal concentration than birds and mammals. Hover over a season for exact values.

05

The Bigger Picture

Places and months with more wildlife observations also tend to reveal more recorded species. This highlights an important feature of citizen-science data: greater observation effort can reveal greater recorded diversity.

Each point represents one state-month combination. The smooth line summarises the overall relationship between observation effort and recorded species richness. Hover over a point for state, climate and observation details.

Final takeaway

What does this tell us?

Australian wildlife records show clear geographic, seasonal and climate-associated patterns. However, observation effort strongly influences what is recorded, so these patterns should not be interpreted as direct measures of wildlife population size or abundance.
